HOW TO USE THE STUDIO

Every sound undertone plays is a patch: a short list of units run once per sample, left to right, on a small stack (up to 8 values). Sources push a value, processors change the top one, and out pops one into the mix. The studio edits that list and plays the result as you go.

WHAT YOU CAN EDIT

All three are patches, which is why they edit alike. What differs is who plays them, and when:

  • shot: a sound effect (a hit, a pickup, a footstep). The game fires it once, on an event, on a degree of the music's scale so it stays in key. It isn't part of the music: mute, solo and the meters leave it alone. Its gate is in milliseconds, since nothing musical times it.
  • lead: the soloist. The music plays it on every melody note. Its gate is in rows, the music's steps (a row is an eighth note).
  • bed palette: the backing band, everything but the melody: a patch each for drone, fifth, pad, air and pulse, and for the drum lanes (kick, clap, shaker). The music plays them all at once, so you edit one track at a time. bass is what a theme's bass lane plays (left alone, the string bass); the pads play it as a bass note under the bed.

A mood ties them together. It is the arrangement: scales and key, tempo, how loud each layer is and how busy the drums are at each level, the room, and which palette and leads to play. The game moves the mood's level (how deep it is) and tension (how dangerous); the seed picks among the mood's choices. So the music is a mood playing its palette and a lead, and the game's shots sound over it.

A FIRST SOUND IN A MINUTE

  1. Press play (or space; m mutes and unmutes) to start the bed (dungeon is a good quiet one).
  2. Pick shot, open tink, and press 1–8: the shot plays on that degree of the bed's scale.
  3. Drag the env unit's decay: every edit fires the shot, so you hear it change as you drag. Shift-click a pad to repeat it while you work; shift-click again (or click any pad) to stop.
  4. + add a unit… → svf, then drag it by its ⠿ grip to just before out: a resonant filter. Turn up res.
  5. Rename it, then download the EDN.

READING A PATCH

The usual voice is env → osc → mul → out: an envelope and an oscillator are pushed, mul multiplies them (the envelope shapes the tone), out mixes it. Two such chains in one patch are two layers. The coloured bars after a unit are the stack after it runs: signal (from an osc, pluck, pluckraw or dust) or control (from an env or lfo). A ! marks a unit the engine refuses (the stack runs dry or overflows there, or a value is out of range); hover it for why.

The strip on a unit's left edge says what kind of unit it is:

  • source, which makes a sound: osc, pluck, pluckraw, dust.
  • modulator, which shapes something over time: env, lfo, scale, pitch.
  • effect, which changes the signal: mul, lpf, hpf, svf, drive, crush, comb, room.
  • output, which sends it on: out, send, verb.
  • modulate: push an lfo or env, then an svf with a nonzero depth pops it to sweep the cutoff; pitch pops it to bend the oscillators after it; scale turns it into a range for a tremolo with mul.
  • grit: drive overdrives, crush lowers the sample rate and bit depth.
  • space: send feeds the tempo delay, verb the room. Both read the top without popping it.

HEARING IT

  • Every edit registers the sound. If the engine refuses it, the status says why and outlines the unit; the last good version keeps playing.
  • A lead or palette you edit replaces the bed's own, so you hear it in the music. solo mutes the rest.
  • mute (on at first) silences the bed (music gain 0) for a shot on its own: the room and delay still ring, and the mood still sets the key its degrees land in. Click it off to hear the music; leads and palettes need it off.
  • Change mood, level, tension, seed to hear it in other keys, tempos and densities. The meters show each layer (not shots).
  • Clicking a name loads it into the editor. A built-in plays as it is; your first edit makes it your own copy (name-2), since built-in names can't be replaced. Shift-click makes a copy straight away. Sounds you registered are in italic.

TAKING IT TO A GAME

The SHOT, LEAD or BED PALETTE EDN box is exactly what the lg binding takes (TAKE EDN holds trigger maps, to audition). Load it at boot, then use the name:

(undertone/register-shot   (read-string (slurp "sfx/zap.edn")))
(undertone/trigger {:shot :zap :degree 2})

(undertone/register-lead    (read-string (slurp "music/glass.edn")))
(undertone/register-palette (read-string (slurp "music/cavern.edn")))
;; a mood names them with :lead and :palette, or set-parameters does

The box takes one back too: edit it, or paste a shot, lead or palette map into it, and it applies after a pause. A new :name asks first: create it, rename the open sound, or cancel.

LIMITS

  • At most 8 values on the stack. A patch needs an env (it ends the sound) and an out.
  • Shots: release up to 2 s, out gain up to 2. Leads and palette patches: release up to 10 s, gain up to 4.
  • Shots aren't part of the music: they don't show on the meters, and they ignore mute and solo.
  • This is a spike: nothing is saved in the page. Download what you want to keep.
