editing

  • Tap a cell to place a note, tap it again to clear; drag to paint. One note per step in each lane.
  • Rows are scale degrees, so the pattern stays in key when the mood changes.
  • Drum cells are sixteenths: a tap cycles hit, accent, ghost, chance, rest.
  • Beside the bed, tension is the game's: as it rises, dotted fills come more often, the mood's busy rises, and the bed tightens. The grid's drums keep their level, and the tempo stays the slider's.
  • A dashed drum cell is a chance step: each time round it plays, softly, at the busy chance, or rests. A drum cell that plays flashes and fades, so a chance step that played fills in for a moment. Dotted is a fill. Most moods' own busy is 0%.
  • alt- or shift-click a cell to hear it without placing it.

playing

  • space plays and stops; r goes back to the first step.
  • Edits are heard the next time the playhead passes them.
  • A pick in the header opens its choices; ← → step through them while it is open, and esc closes it.
  • A bass voice plays at the level of the pluck it replaces.
  • Every note echoes 3 steps later, and again every 3, each fainter: an echo can sound like a note of its own.
  • With bed on, the mood's drone, fifth and pad play under the grid, and their chord moves each pass (tonic, then VI or iv) while the grid's rows stay on the key's root, so one loop can sound different from pass to pass.

seeing

  • Each lane's strip follows the playhead: ticks are note starts, over the lane's level before the echo; the last pass stays dim.
  • mix shows all four lanes on one strip, their colours added.
  • echo shows what the echo returns, all lanes' at once, scaled to its loudest, with a tick where each echo arrives: a bump some steps after a hit is that hit coming back.
  • edn shows the pattern as EDN, a theme's grid, which can be edited or pasted over.
